## What the table shows 

Each floor is right for its own kind of search and wrong for the other. The residual floor is matched to
margin-scored search. Built per instance (A, fully paired; B, economy), its excess over the measured
hackability includes 0 in all 12 margin-scored groups (mean +0.0025 and +0.0017, largest |excess| 0.050).
Built across the task family (every scaling tried), it excludes 0 in 3 or 4 of 12. There it is too high at
large budgets (cem B = 1,000: -0.090 to -0.110) and too low for best-of-n at B = 1 (up to +0.075), so family
pooling is the weak point, not the residual floor itself. The flip floor is matched to verdict-scored random
search. With per-instance base rates and confusion rates pooled within the task family, it includes 0 in all
4 verdict-scored groups (mean -0.031; identical in every run, since it does not depend on the residual pool).
Crossed, both fail:
- The flip floor sits up to 0.305 above margin-scored search at B >= 10 (excess -0.305 to +0.08 over the
  12 groups).
- The residual floor sits 0.07 to 0.12 below verdict-scored search at B >= 10 (excess +0.120, +0.070,
  +0.095 at B = 10, 100, 1,000, every interval above 0).

## Rule A diagnostics: seed spread of selected against pool plans 

The seed spread is the standard deviation of the engine margin over the nine verdict seeds (primary and
eight secondary), averaged over the 200 instances. It is reported for:

What it shows:
- **Real search is variance-seeking.** Where search exerts pressure, the plans it selects have more
  seed-to-seed spread than the pool: at B = 1,000, 0.236 for cem, 0.250 for best-of-n and 0.212 for
  random, against 0.203.
- **The floor goes the other way** (0.175 to 0.19 at B >= 100). Its residual is drawn independently of the
  plan, so it can climb only through a plan's nine-seed mean, and high-mean plans are the calmer ones.
- **The floor's gain comes from the drawn residual.** It reaches its score with larger residuals (+0.44 to
  +0.55 at B = 1,000) than the real search's own (+0.27 to +0.40), which gains more through the plan
  itself.

At 200 instances the two effects roughly cancel in the excess (no group significant, largest 0.050). The
mechanism is real, however: the engine's noise spread depends on the plan, and a residual drawn
independently of the plan cannot reproduce search that seeks variance. For an emulator whose sampling
spread depends on the plan, the residual floor is a null for plan-independent error. It does not cover
plan-dependent noise, and the paper should say so beside every excess.
